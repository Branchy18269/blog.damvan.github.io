Are Blockchains Still Relevant?

2026-01-21  |  Dalton Goodwin-Peck
Leland Jensen

Is blockchain still used in major industries today? Yes. Companies like Walmart use blockchain to track products from where they started to the store shelf. This lets them track items instantly if there is a recall or an investigation is needed. Blockchain is also used for digital ownership (NFTs). This lets players own and trade game items in different marketplaces. However, this has been up and down because a lot of people don’t like the extra costs and that it ruins the game.

Has interest in blockchain grown, stayed the same, or declined? It has grown as a useful tool, but there is much less hype from the public.

  • US Growth: Crypto ownership rose slightly to roughly 30% of adults in 2026 (up from 27% in 2024).
  • Big Money is Moving In: The approval of Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs in the US made the tech official for Wall Street, leading to huge amounts of money flowing in.
  • Calmer Market: The crazy times of covid (crazy NFT prices, meme coins) have calmed down. Growth now comes from business tasks like making payments and settling contracts.

What problems does blockchain still solve effectively? Blockchain is great for solving problems where strangers need to trust each other. It allows a secure user to send money or prove ownership directly to another person without needing a bank or middleman to check the transaction.

  • Transparency & Tracking: In supply chains, it creates a permanent record of a product's journey that no one can hide.
  • Security & Permanent Records: Once data is on a blockchain, it is extremely hard to change. This is vital for money records, legal documents, and ID checks.
Interesting Insight for Blockchains: When Walmart tested tracking sliced mangoes using their old paper methods, it took them nearly 7 days to trace a package back to the specific farm it came from. When they switched to blockchain, they performed the exact same search in just 2.2 seconds.

What challenges or criticisms does blockchain face now? Despite progress, significant hurdles remain in 2025-2026:

  • Speed & Cost: Popular blockchains like Ethereum can get clogged up when too many people use them at once. This causes transaction fees to skyrocket. While new updates are helping to lower costs, using blockchain is still often more expensive than using standard computer systems.
  • Regulation: While improving, regulation is fragmented globally. Agencies like the CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission) face capacity crises in overseeing these markets, and tax laws in many nations remain unclear.
  • Incompatibilities: Different blockchains still struggle to "talk" to each other securely without using "bridges," which are frequent targets for hackers.
  • User Experience: Managing private keys and wallets is still too complex for the average non-technical person. If you lose your key, you lose your assets a scary feature for a lot of people.
  • Environmental Concerns: While Ethereum switched to a green model, Bitcoin's high energy usage remains a point of criticism for environmental groups.

In my opinion, is blockchain still relevant? YES! Why?
Blockchain survived the big hype cycle (it didn't die). It is no longer just looking for a problem to solve; it is the foundation for the future of finance and digital IDs. You might not see the word "Blockchain" on every product, but it is becoming a huge part of the digital economy. The fact that the world's biggest money managers and governments are involved proves it is here to stay.
