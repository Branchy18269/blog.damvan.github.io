Blog Post 4 | Password Security

2026-04-13  |  Leland Jensen, Will Hyndman, Dalton Goodwin-Peck

Password Security and the Rising Wave of Cyber Threats: The Cybersecurity Challenge Facing Us in 2026, password security is no longer optional; it is an integral part of protecting ourselves from cyberattacks and ensuring the safety of our personal information and online assets. With hackers and malicious actors employing various methods to compromise user accounts, including credential stuffing and ransomware, password security is more relevant than ever before. The Problem with Passwords: They're Still Our Biggest Weakness Even after several decades of warning people about the dangers of weak passwords, we've found out that 94% of people use the same password across multiple websites and services. This vulnerability makes them susceptible to credential stuffing attacks, which are the leading type of attack in 2026, accounting for 31% of all cyberattacks.

Data Breaches: The Cybersecurity Trend That Will Not Slow Down According to cybersecurity professionals, there's been a rapid increase in the rate of breaches and their consequences across different fields: Ransomware now leads the list of causes for all data breaches (44%), a jump of 12% compared to last year. Small businesses face a data breach once every 11 seconds because of insufficient password protection and failure to implement MFA. Breaches among government institutions have increased by threefold compared to 2020 because of insufficient identity management and control. Breaches in the supply chain industry have become four times more frequent due to attackers compromising credentials of employees who can access other organizations' accounts.

Why Are Passwords So Important? Passwords are still the most common method used to prove identity, but they can hardly provide sufficient security by themselves. Hackers employ artificial intelligence to guess passwords and continue sending phishing emails to trick users into providing sensitive credentials. As a result, data breaches in 2026 mostly involve compromised passwords and social engineering techniques.
